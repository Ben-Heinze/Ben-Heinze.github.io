* Assignment 2: Synchronized Typed Buffer

Handout: [[file:Assignment_2_Typed_Buffer.pdf][Assignment 2: Typed Buffer (PDF)]] (the Markdown version,
=assignment-2-typed-buffer.md=, is kept alongside it in this folder). Due 11/01/2026.

*Topic:* thread synchronization --- a variation of the [[file:../lectures/2-2-process-management/index.org::*Producer/Consumer Problem][bounded producer/consumer problem]]
in which each consumer can only take items of its own type.

** Purpose

The assignment exercises the ideas from the mutual exclusion and concurrency
problems lectures on a real thread library (POSIX threads):

- Protect shared state with a /mutex/ so every operation on it is atomic and free of {{{hl(green,race conditions)}}}
- Make a thread /block on a predicate/ (a condition about the shared state) with
  condition variables or semaphores, instead of {{{hl(red,busy waiting)}}}
- Block and resume correctly: {{{hl(green,no lost wakeups)}}}, and no thread left blocked
- Argue /liveness/: the design cannot deadlock, livelock, or leave a thread blocked at the end
- Make each thread's output reproducible even though the interleaving is not

** How it works

- A /bounded buffer/ of capacity *50* is shared by *two producers* and *two consumers*, all threads
- Items are integers 1--100, tagged *odd* or *even*
  - =producer_odd= inserts random odd values; =producer_even= inserts random even values
  - =consumer_odd= removes only odd items; =consumer_even= removes only even items
- Each producer makes exactly *100* items; each consumer removes exactly *100*
- The buffer tracks three quantities: =size=, =odd_count=, =even_count= --- exactly what the wait conditions test

** The three wait conditions

The twist that makes this a synchronization problem: there are *three distinct
predicates* over one buffer.

| Thread          | Blocks until...                  | Predicate             |
|-----------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------|
| Either producer | there is free space              | =size < 50=           |
| =consumer_odd=  | an /odd/ item is present         | =odd_count > 0=       |
| =consumer_even= | an /even/ item is present        | =even_count > 0=      |

A consumer waits on the presence of /its own type/, not on "buffer not empty".
A buffer full of even items must not wake =consumer_odd=.

*Why one "not empty" condition is not enough:* it cannot tell an odd-waiter from
an even-waiter. With a single shared condition variable and a single =signal=,
the /wrong/ consumer can be woken (it finds nothing it can take and goes back to
sleep) while the consumer that /could/ proceed keeps sleeping. Because a
condition variable {{{hl(red,does not remember a signal)}}}, that wakeup is lost
for good: an effective deadlock.

** Key synchronization ideas

- *Mutual exclusion and check-and-act:* "is there an odd item? if so remove one"
  must be a /single/ critical section. Checking in one critical section and
  removing in another is a {{{hl(red,time-of-check to time-of-use (TOCTOU))}}}
  bug: the item can vanish in between.
- *No busy waiting:* a thread that cannot proceed blocks on a condition variable
  or semaphore rather than spinning.
- *=while=, not =if=, around a condition wait:* when a thread resumes it must
  re-check its predicate, because the state may have changed again (or the
  wakeup was spurious). Re-checking in a loop around a blocking wait is not busy
  waiting.
- *No lost wakeups:* any operation that makes a waiting thread's predicate true
  must let that thread resume. Inserting an item can wake the matching consumer;
  removing an item can wake a producer waiting for space.
  - A /counting semaphore/ remembers a signal that arrives before anyone waits
  - A /condition variable/ forgets it, so signals must be targeted correctly (or use =broadcast=)
- *Targeted blocking:* each waiter should block on the /specific/ condition it
  needs --- e.g. separate semaphores (or condition variables) for
  /odd-available/, /even-available/, and /space-available/. Funneling everyone
  through one condition and waking all waiters on every change is correct but
  wasteful.
  - The program counts /wasted wakeups/: times a thread resumes only to find it
    still cannot proceed. Targeted blocking keeps this near zero.
- *Wait on the resource before the mutex:* with semaphores, a producer must wait
  on /space/ first and then on the mutex. Taking the mutex first and then
  blocking on /space/ freezes everyone --- the same
  [[file:../lectures/2-2-process-management/index.org::*Questions][fatal swap from the producer/consumer lecture]].

** Termination

- Producers stop after their 100 items; consumers stop after removing 100 items
- The fixed count is exact because of the type invariant: only =consumer_odd=
  removes odd items, so it consumes exactly what =producer_odd= made
- Terminating a consumer on "buffer empty and producers finished" instead {{{hl(red,can hang)}}}
- At the end all four threads exit cleanly, with none left blocked, and =main= joins them

** Reproducibility

- Each producer's /sequence of values/ is reproducible for a given seed (default 42),
  because each has its /own/ pseudo-random generator state seeded from the base seed
- Sharing one generator (e.g. =rand()=) across threads is not thread-safe, and its
  output would depend on the interleaving
- The /interleaving/ (the order operations happen across threads) is still
  nondeterministic --- that is expected

** Logging

- Each thread writes its own log file; every line records the action
  (=before_insert=, =after_insert=, =before_remove=, =after_remove=), the value,
  and =size=/=odd=/=even= at that instant
- The /before/ snapshot, the change, and the /after/ snapshot are taken atomically under the buffer lock
- A global =seq= number, incremented inside the critical section, lets all four
  logs be merged into one consistent order of operations
