The first deck is a single-processor treatment: the three timescales a scheduler
operates on, the criteria it is judged by, and six classic short-term policies run
against one shared example. The second deck takes the same question to two real
systems and shows that neither of them uses a textbook policy verbatim. The last
two decks move from /which/ process runs to what happens when processes running
together share things: mutual exclusion and synchronization, then deadlock and
starvation.
