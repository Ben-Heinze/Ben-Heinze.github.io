A scaffolded project: outline an analysis plan for one component of a research
study, and develop language for communicating its design and eventual results
with an appropriate scope of inference. My project analyzes Coe et al.,
/Impact of cognitive training on claims-based diagnosed dementia over 20 years:
evidence from the ACTIVE study/, which linked the ACTIVE cognitive-training trial
to Medicare claims data to get a 20-year follow-up on Alzheimer's disease and
related dementias (ADRD).

Readings: Chapters 23 and 24 of /The Statistical Sleuth/, and Higgs & Amrhein
(2025), /Giving Less Power to Statistical Power/ (on the [[file:part-2/index.org][Part 2]] page).

* Assignment

** Part I (April 6, 5pm)

Provide background to contextualize a single component of the research, along
with a potential analysis plan.

- *Background:* a short paragraph describing the research context and relevant
  background information to motivate general research objectives.
- *Description of study design:* describe the design using language from
  chapters 23/24 and create a conceptual diagram (e.g., the seaweed grazer or
  soybean diagrams). Use the diagram to aid the description.
- *Research question:* the question to investigate and a tentative plan for
  using regression (or a GLM). It can be compound (additivity, pre-planned
  contrasts, etc.) but should have no more than 3 major components.
- *Proposed theoretical model, EDA plan, and analysis plan:* can be in shorthand
  Sleuth notation, with the measurement structure if an LMM is needed, plus a
  bulleted list of raw-data visualizations and the steps for using the model to
  address the question. For a two-way ANOVA, for example:
  - Pirateplot of the raw data and an interaction plot of group averages ± sd.
  - ESS F-test comparing additive and non-additive models, plus research
    context, to assess the "additivity" assumption.
  - Summarize the final inferential model with ESS F-tests, effects plots,
    and/or linear contrasts.
  - /Descriptions should be in the context of the research study!/
- *Scope of inference:* a few sentences on the scope of inference the design
  affords, using hypothetical results in context (e.g., because TRT was not
  randomly assigned to U, differences in mean Y among TRT indicate associations
  between TRT and Y).

** Part II (April 13, 5pm)

- Read Higgs and Amrhein (2025) and Sleuth 23.4-23.5. In your own words, translate
  the cautions in Higgs and Amrhein to the content of 23.4 and 23.5: what
  "baggage" comes with the Sleuth's power calculation methods? (Max 1/2 page
  double spaced.)
- *Quantitative backdrop:* draw one for a parameter in the research question
  that could be summarized with a confidence interval, with labeled intervals
  and the practical implications of each.
- Pick /one/ interval from the backdrop and write a /hypothetical/ summary of
  statistical findings, with scope of inference and practical context.

** Part III (April 20, 5pm)

Add the quantitative backdrop and summary of statistical findings to a single
"analysis plan" document, incorporating instructor feedback so far. Peer review
was due April 27 and the final report May 6 (5% bonus before May 1).

** Rubric

Each section is scored on a 10-point scale: 0-1 no/little credit, 2-5
beginning, 6-8 developing, 9-10 advanced. The analysis plan document (90 points):

1. *Introduction (10):* overview of the research area and objectives, and a clearly stated research question.
2. *Methods (30):*
   - Field methods / study design (10): thorough description, a concept diagram, the research question.
   - Proposed statistical procedures (10): a theoretical model with all variables and parameters of interest
     defined; a description and /sketch/ of at least one exploratory plot; a plan for refining the model.
   - Quantitative backdrop (10): a figure with intervals leading to different conclusions, a full caption,
     and a one- or two-sentence description.
3. *Hypothetical results / summary of statistical findings (20):* communicating the model-refinement steps
   and a hypothetical summary for a chosen interval (10); at least one sketched figure for summarizing
   results, e.g. an effects plot (10).
4. *Scope of inference (10).*
5. *Conclusion (10):* practical implications of the chosen interval using the backdrop, and next steps.
6. *Overall quality (10):* organization and complete sentences (5), spelling and grammar (2.5), following the format (2.5).
