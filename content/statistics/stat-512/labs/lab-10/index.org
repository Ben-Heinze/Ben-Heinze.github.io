Consider a designed experiment to investigate the effects of increasing concentrations of gaseous carbon disulphide (mg/l) on confused flour beetles (Strand, 1930). Approximately 30 confused flour beetles were put into a container where they were exposed to a concentration of gas for 5 hrs. The number of dead beetles per container and concentration of the gas was recorded at the end of 5 hrs.  Dosages were randomly assigned to containers. The experiment was repeated twice, providing two counts of dead beetles out 30 at each concentration. They recorded how many beetles died (~y~) out of the total (~n~) with rep 1 and rep 2 denoting the two runs at each concentration. Note that their variable ~n~ is . You can ignore the ~rep~ variable available in the data set although it could be a source of "extra-binomial variation".

/Source/: Strand, A. (1930) Measuring the toxicity of insect fumigants. /Industrial and Engineering Chemistry: analytical edition/, 2(1), 4-8.

*Variables*

- ~conc~: Concentration of carbon disulphide after 5 hrs (mg/l)
- ~y~: Number of dead beetles in concentration group 
- ~n~: Total number of beetles in each concentration group (like our $m_i$ in our Binomial logistic regression notation).  
- ~rep~: Replicate number (2 trials for each concentration group)

library(patchwork)
range(df_beetle$conc)
# create new data frame with concentrations ranging from 
# min conc to max conc
new_x <- data.frame(conc = seq(from = 49.06, to = 76.54,
                               length.out = 100))
# find the estimated mean and se for mean on the logit scale
est_logit <- predict(fit_death, type = "link", 
                    newdata = new_x, se.fit = TRUE) 

# put in DF and make intervals, prob scale estimates, and fit type 
df_pred <- tibble(est = est_logit$fit, se = est_logit$se.fit) |> 
  # approx 95% ci and prob scale ests
  mutate(est_lower = est - 2*se, 
         est_upper = est + 2*se, 
         est_prob = exp(est)/(1 + exp(est)), 
         prob_lower = exp(est_lower)/(1 + exp(est_lower)), 
         prob_upper = exp(est_upper)/(1 + exp(est_upper)), 
         fit_type = "linear", 
         conc = new_x$conc
         )
# take a look at the df 
head(df_pred)

# now make similar df using the quadratic model..

                    newdata = new_x, se.fit = TRUE)
