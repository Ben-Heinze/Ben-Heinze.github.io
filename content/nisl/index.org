* 1 Intro to Probability Theory

** 1.1.2 Basic Concepts in Probability Theory

In Bayesian formalism, belief measures obey three basic axioms of probability calculus:

1. \(0 \leq P(A) \leq 1\)

2. \(P(\text{sure proposition}) = 1\)

3. \(P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) \text{ if } A \text{ and } B \text{ are mutually exclusive} \)

*Law of Total Probability*: In a joint probability, you can sum over the states of an event to /marginalize/ that probability. 
For example, if we are calculating $P(A)$ from $P\left( A, B \right)$, and $B$ has binary states, we can marginalize over the states of $B$:


\[
  P(A) = P\left( A, B \right) + P\left( A, \neg B \right) \text{ where } P\left( A,B \right) \text{ is short for } P\left( A \wedge B \right)
\]

If B had more than two states, we can some over all of them to marginalize $B$ of the joint probability:

\[
  P\left( A \right) = \sum_{i=1}^{} P\left( A, B_{i} \right)
\]
