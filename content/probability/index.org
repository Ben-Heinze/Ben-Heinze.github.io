* Probability

  /Probability provides a way of summarizing the uncertainty that comes from our laziness and ignorance/

  A *probability* is a number between $0$ and $1$ that measures how
  likely an event is to occur, given the available evidence. For an
  event $A$,

  \[
  0 \le P(A) \le 1
  \]

  with $P(A) = 1$ meaning $A$ is certain, and $P(A) = 0$ meaning $A$
  is impossible. Probabilities over all possible outcomes of a random
  variable must sum (or integrate) to $1$.

  /utility theory/ says that every state (or state sequence) has a degree of usefulness,or utility,to an agent and that the agent will prefer states with higher utility.

* Prior Probability
  *Prior Probability* (unconditional probability), refer to degrees of belief in propositions /in the absence of any other information/.

  The prior probability of a random variable $X$ taking value $x$ is
  written $P(X = x)$, or just $P(x)$. For example, $P(\text{Cavity} =
  \text{true})$ is our belief that a patient has a cavity /before/
  we observe any symptoms or test results.

* Conditional Probability

  *Conditional Probability* is the probability of one event $A$
  /given/ that another event $B$ is known to have occurred. It is
  written $P(A \mid B)$ and defined as

  \[
  P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(A, B)}{P(B)}, \quad P(B) > 0
  \]

  i.e. the fraction of $B$'s probability mass that also satisfies
  $A$. For example, $P(\text{Toothache} \mid \text{Cavity})$ is the
  probability a patient has a toothache, /given/ that they have a
  cavity.

* Marginal Probability

  *Marginal Probability* is the probability of a single variable,
  obtained by summing (or integrating) a joint distribution over all
  values of the other variables — this is called /marginalizing out/
  the other variables. For discrete variables $X$ and $Y$,

  \[
  P(X = x) = \sum_{y} P(X = x, Y = y)
  \]

  For example, if we know the joint distribution $P(\text{Cavity},
  \text{Toothache})$, we can recover $P(\text{Cavity})$ by summing
  over both values of $\text{Toothache}$.

* Likelihood

  *Likelihood* is the same conditional-probability expression as
  above, $P(B \mid A)$, but read in the other direction: instead of
  treating $A$ as fixed and asking how likely the evidence $B$ is,
  we fix the observed evidence $B$ and ask how plausible different
  values of $A$ are.

  See [[file:../statistics/likelihood/index.org][Likelihood]] in
  Statistics for the full definition, Maximum Likelihood Estimation,
  and the distinction between likelihood and probability.

* Posterior Probability

  *Posterior Probability* is a [[*Conditional Probability][conditional probability]] — the updated
  degree of belief in a proposition /after/ taking some evidence
  into account. The posterior probability of $A$ given evidence $B$
  is written $P(A \mid B)$.

  Using Bayes' rule, the posterior can be computed from the prior
  $P(A)$ and the likelihood $P(B \mid A)$:

  \[
  P(A \mid B) = \frac{P(B \mid A)\,P(A)}{P(B)}
  \]

  For example, $P(\text{Cavity} \mid \text{Toothache})$ is our
  updated belief that a patient has a cavity, /after/ learning that
  they have a toothache.
